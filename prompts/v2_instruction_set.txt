- Lexical, phonetic, and semantic similarity must be evaluated independently
- Phonetic similarity ≥ 0.85 counts as strong similarity
- Strong phonetic similarity can independently drive confusion risk
- Do not average similarity blindly; interpret signals


RELATED GOODS / ADJACENCY LOGIC (CRITICAL)

- Do NOT hardcode specific added terms such as “laces,” “shoes,” “apparel,” “cafe,” etc.
- Do NOT use one-off word triggers to raise or lower risk

- When a new filing contains a similar core mark plus added wording:
  - Identify the core mark
  - Classify added wording as:
    - descriptive / generic modifier
    - category or product descriptor
    - potentially distinctive element

- If added wording is descriptive or generic:
  → do NOT allow it to materially reduce confusion risk by itself


RELATED-GOODS FRAMEWORK

Treat goods as commercially adjacent when one or more apply:

- goods function as complements, accessories, components, or line extensions
- goods are commonly sold through the same channels
- goods target the same purchasers or usage context
- consumers could reasonably assume shared brand origin
- goods are marketed, used, or encountered together
- goods proximity is strong enough that class differences should not control


RISK LOGIC

HIGH
- Near-identical mark (lexical OR phonetic) + same class
- Strong similarity (lexical OR phonetic) + adjacent goods
- Similar mark + strong behavioral signal

MEDIUM
- Moderate similarity + somewhat related goods
- Mixed context

LOW
- Partial OR high similarity + unrelated goods
- No behavioral signal


RISK APPLICATION RULES

- Descriptive or generic added wording must NOT reduce risk when core-mark similarity is strong and goods are commercially adjacent
- Absence of behavioral signal must NOT reduce risk in cases of strong similarity + adjacent goods
- Strong phonetic similarity (≥ 0.85) combined with commercially adjacent goods MUST be classified as HIGH risk unless clear affiliation signals exist
- In cases of strong similarity + adjacent goods, MEDIUM risk must NOT be assigned

- Adjacent goods override class differences in risk determination
- Strong core-mark similarity combined with adjacent goods is sufficient for HIGH risk classification and must not be downgraded
- Descriptive added wording has no material impact on risk when core similarity is strong
- Similarity must always be stated truthfully, regardless of final risk level
- Final risk determination is driven by context, but context must NOT override HIGH classification when strong similarity and adjacency are present

- Absence of behavioral signal does not negate risk if similarity + adjacency are strong
